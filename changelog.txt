Unreleased

- Added the FIDE Swiss Team Pairing System (C.04.6), including all three colour
  models of article 1.7 - type A colour preferences, type B colour preferences,
  and no colour preferences at all - automatic TRF selection, and team-corpus
  coverage.
- Routed all 1,000 team fixtures through the new engine and removed the 697
  expected failures that represented the previously missing C.04.6 implementation;
  every record in the 6,000-record corpus executes in CI.
- Corrected seven faults in the C.04.6 pairing that change the pairing or the
  colours a team tournament gets. The tournament pairing number is now the
  team's fixed place in the field (articles 1.1.1 and 1.1.3) rather than a
  running count that an absent team handed down, which had reversed the colours
  of every team below an absence through article 4.3.1. A bracket is read in
  tournament-pairing-number order when its pairing identifier is formed
  (article 3.6.1), so heterogeneous brackets are no longer identified wrongly.
  The first-team is decided on the standings score (article 4.2.1) instead of
  the accelerated score, so virtual points no longer settle colours. The drawn
  initial colour is recovered through article 4.3.1. Two teams can no longer be
  made to meet twice by the maxMeets option, which article 2.1.1 [C1] does not
  allow. Board order tie-breaks on the tournament pairing number, and the
  colour model and the secondary score are each resolved from one place, so
  naming a model on the command line and naming it in record 192 agree.
  Naming the primary score on the command line (-m fideteam-mp) no longer
  discards the file's answer on the secondary score: a FIDE_TEAM_TYPEA_MP file,
  which states that game points are not used, keeps that answer and article
  4.2.2 stays off. Only a command line naming both scores overrides it. In
  check mode, a declared pairing-allocated bye given to a team that article
  2.1.2 [C2] bars from it is flagged on the bye bracket of the analysis, naming
  the team and the article.
- Replaced the invented round the team engine produced when no legal pairing
  existed. It used to pair as many teams as it could and hand a bye to every
  team left over, which gives out byes article 1.4 has none to give and repeats
  byes article 2.1.2 [C2] forbids. Such a round is now reported as unpairable,
  which article 3.3.3 leaves to the Chief Arbiter to decide.
- Refused a team tournament whose length is not declared within two rounds of
  the end the reader inferred, for every colour model and in both modes.
  Articles 2.3.4 [C7] and 2.3.7 [C10] apply "with the exception of the last two
  rounds", and a file without record 142 does not say which rounds those are:
  the reader infers the count from the last round played, so every round it was
  asked for looked like one of the last two and the two criteria were switched
  off on a guess. Pairing such a round, or checking the last two played rounds
  of such a file, reports status 401 naming record 142 and the -N flag, which
  lifts it; for a type B file the message also names article 1.7.2, whose
  last-round clauses need the same answer. Earlier rounds are unaffected.
- Corrected the reported C.04.6 [C5], [C7] and [C10] quality of a pair
  containing two upfloaters. Both teams are below the score level of the
  bracket, so both scores and previous-round floats must be counted, and each
  is the other upfloater's opponent. This does not change a reachable pairing:
  [C4] ensures a selected bracket cannot pair two of its own upfloaters, because
  dropping that pair would leave a legal round with two fewer upfloaters. It
  makes the checker and diagnostics report the criteria themselves correctly.
- Enforced article 1.1.1 on record 310 for the FIDE Swiss team pairing system:
  team tournament pairing numbers must be distinct and cover the complete range
  from 1 through the number of teams. Other team systems, such as a team round
  robin, need distinct numbers only, as TRF26 record 310 asks. The reader stops
  after the first refused record, so a non-numeric team pairing number is
  reported once as 401 naming the line instead of as 502 with that message
  buried under two more.
  Duplicate, zero and gapped identifiers are now rejected at the record instead
  of reaching array indexing or silently replacing a team.
- Corrected Sonneborn-Berger cut modifiers to select a voluntary unplayed
  round by its contribution, and to break a tie between equal contributions
  by the opponent's score, as required by FIDE C.07 article 16.5. Affects
  tournaments starting on or after 1 March 2026 only.
- Capped the dummy opponent of an unplayed round at the points for a draw times
  the number of rounds in the tournament (C.07 article 16.4.2), the scheduled
  rounds rather than the round the standings are taken after. Intermediate
  standings under the 2026 rules were capped as if the event ended at the
  current round. Final standings are unchanged.
- Added a tie-break value baseline over the TRF corpus, holding a broad
  catalogue of 32 individual and 38 team tie-break calculations for every
  competitor under both the 2024 and the 2026 rules.
- Fixed absolute colour preferences when the colour difference is +/-1 and the
  player received the same colour in their last two played games.
- The COP tie-break lists the colour preference of C.04.3 article 1.7 exactly
  as the pairing engine computes it. The listing had a notation of its own that
  read the colour off the colour difference alone, so a player on wwwbb was
  listed as due Black where the engine gives an absolute preference for White,
  and it emitted strengths the article does not define. Engine and listing now
  share one function. The tie-break value baseline moves on 94 corpus records,
  all of them the deliberately invalid colour fixtures ind_04258_inv_c3 to
  ind_04999_inv_c3.
- Added typed engine errors and fixed crashes involving unpairable fields,
  incomplete results, uncommon scoring systems, invalid pairing numbers,
  long colour sequences, and tie-break cuts. A Dutch round that cannot be
  completed is reported with status 505 and a message naming how many
  competitors would remain unpaired (C.04.3 article 1.9.3) instead of being
  returned as an empty pairing with status 0. A team tournament declared by
  record 192 with a player section and no record 310 is refused by name, record
  310 being mandatory for rating and pairing in TRF26, instead of failing with a
  KeyError and status 502. A team whose first player has a start number of 1000
  or more is no longer refused as misaligned data. A second record 320, or a
  second record 240 of the same type and round, is refused naming the earlier
  line instead of silently replacing it. Ctrl-C while a file is read is no
  longer reported as an error in the file.
- Added a pytest regression suite, a 6,000-record TRF corpus, and automated
  testing on Python 3.11 and 3.14 with GitHub Actions. Pull requests receive a
  single, automatically updated comment summarising the complete test matrix.
  The suite also runs on every push to main.
- Removed reachable debug breakpoints from the pairing engine and replaced
  invariant checks with explicit errors.
- Fixed parsing of TRF26 record 299 abnormal assignments. A record with no round
  and no pairing numbers states what an abnormal result is worth for the whole
  tournament and is applied as before. Applying an assignment scoped to a round
  or to a competitor remains future work, so a file whose scoped assignment would
  change a result the engine reports is now rejected, with a message naming the
  record, the scope and the two point values, instead of being accepted and then
  scored by rules other than the ones it asked for. A scoped assignment that
  cannot change anything - because no recorded result matches the one it names,
  or because it agrees with the score system already in force - is read and
  accepted as before. A record 299 with a blank type - TRF26's "penalty/bonus
  points (may be negative)", which modify the points in the standings - is
  refused when it carries points, scoped or not, with a message naming the
  record, the scope and the points, because the engine does not yet apply a
  standings adjustment; such a file used to be read with status 0 and ranked
  without it. A blank-type record of 0.0 is accepted and inert, and neither form
  reaches the score system any more. A scoped record 299 for a team is checked
  on its game points as well as its match points.
- Corrected the TRF26 record 310 team-total check. The rounds it counts are read
  off the match list, so a last round decided entirely by forfeit is counted and a
  bye announced for a round that has not been played is not. A file that really
  does disagree with its own results is still rejected, and the message now names
  the team, the rounds counted, and the declared and calculated figures.
- Fixed a KeyError on a game recorded from only one side, such as a
  pairing-allocated bye whose opponent never recorded the same game.
- A TRF record the reader cannot parse is again reported as "Error in trf-file,
  line N" instead of failing later with a TypeError.
- Enforced TRF26 record 320 as the team-only pairing-allocated-bye record the
  format defines. A valid team record now reaches the match list, while an
  individual file carrying record 320 is rejected with a message naming the
  record instead of faulting later in the reader.
- Required record 352's board-colour sequence before pairing the first round of
  a team tournament. Record 310 lists the whole squad, including reserves, so
  its roster length is not the number of boards in a match; a missing board
  count is now rejected clearly instead of silently using the squad size.
- Corrected the documented minimum Python version to 3.11.

Changes in calculation:
Decimal numbers has changed from float to Decimal. 
It's known that 1.1 + 2.2 = is evaluated to3.3000000000000003 in python float.
With Decimal, Decimal('1.1') + Decimal('2.2') is evaluated to Decimal('3.3').
The tiebreak structure has a new element precision, that shows the number of digits after decimal point.

Program:
New switches:
-p : tournament has predefined pairing
-s : tournament is a swiss tournament
-r : sort in rank order (in combination with -d) 
Program returns %errorlevel% 0 if everything is OK
Program returns %errorlevel% 1 with -c switch and rank order does not match
Program returns %errorlevel% >=500 on error

Internal structure:
The sections playerSection and teamSection are replaced by
gameList, matchList, gameScoreSystem, matchScoreSystem and competitors.
competitors are players for individual tournaments and teams for team tournaments.

In return struture
result is changed to tieBreakResult
